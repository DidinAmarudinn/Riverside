# Experiment results: forecast A vs B

All data is synthetic. Generated by `python -m app.forecast.experiment` on 2026-10-16; 642 store-SKU series, true demand from the simulator, `ask_rate_hat` estimated from each experiment's own training data.

## Summary

- **Plain backtest (train to day 76, predict 14 days):** over all 642 series WAPE is 57.5% for A and 57.5% for B (+0.05 points, 95% interval -0.01 to +0.13): there is no clear difference (the interval includes zero). Only about 0.4% of demand is hidden by stockouts in this dataset, and ARIMA_PLUS already absorbs short dips, so A and B are almost the same series and the replayed order policies give the same empty-shelf days. On the 9 series out of stock on the cutoff day, A scores 88.7% and B 89.5%: A is closer on these 9 series, but 9 series cannot support a statistical claim; read it as an illustration.
- **Stress test (stockouts still going on when the forecast is made; 7 days of truth left):** on the 3 series out of stock on the cutoff day, WAPE is 79.2% for A and 58.0% for B (+21.2 points); B is closer on these 3 series, but 3 series cannot support a statistical claim; read it as an illustration. A's bias is -70.6% and B's -21.7%: A reads the empty shelf as vanished demand, B partly corrects it.
- **A trivial forecast (mean of the last 28 days of POS) scores 56.0% over all series, slightly better than both ARIMA_PLUS variants (57.5% and 57.5%).** The gain of B over A should be read against that: it shows B is not a stronger forecaster in general, only that it does not collapse after a long stockout.
- **Coconut-milk scenario (production forecast):** A expects 2.6 units over the next 7 days and orders 10; B expects 52.0 and orders 64. Both come from the formula.
- **Sensitivity:** the ask rate is estimated, not known. Section 2 re-runs B with it shifted by -50% and +50%.

## 3. The ask-rate estimate

`ask_rate_hat` is the share of unmet shoppers that show up as a kiosk signal. It is estimated from the training data only: per category, total unmet signals divided by the unmet demand expected on stockout days (from in-stock days of the same weekday within 28 days), pooled across categories when a category has too little evidence. Estimate for the 90-day production run:

| Category | ask_rate_hat | Unmet signals | Expected unmet units | Source |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Baby | 0.47 | 3 | 1 | pooled |
| Bakery | 0.47 | 0 | 10 | pooled |
| Beverages | 0.47 | 9 | 6 | pooled |
| Cooking Essentials | 0.42 | 70 | 168 | category |
| Dairy & Eggs | 0.55 | 17 | 31 | category |
| Fresh Produce | 0.38 | 24 | 62 | category |
| Frozen | 0.47 | 2 | 0 | pooled |
| Household | 0.47 | 6 | 8 | pooled |
| Instant Food | 0.47 | 0 | 15 | pooled |
| Meat & Seafood | 0.47 | 1 | 0 | pooled |
| Personal Care | 0.47 | 5 | 0 | pooled |
| Snacks | 0.47 | 4 | 1 | pooled |
| ALL | 0.47 | 141 | 302 | pooled |

For validation only (never an input): the generator's own kiosk capture rate is 0.35. The pooled estimate is 0.47 for all 90 days, 0.56 up to day 83 and 0.69 up to day 76 (thin evidence). The estimate runs high: the demand expected from the days around a stockout is an average, while days that end out of stock tend to be high-demand days, so B infers somewhat less lost demand than there really was.

## 4. The coconut-milk scenario (production forecast, made on the last data day)

| | Forecast next 7 days | Order from the formula |
|---|---:|---:|
| A (POS only) | 2.6 | 10 |
| B (POS + unmet) | 52.0 | 64 |

S01 / SKU-0001 had 0 units on hand and 17 unmet shopper requests in the last 7 days. Both orders come from `max(0, forecast + safety stock - on hand - on order)`, not from fixed values. A still orders 10 because its prediction interval is wide after 14 empty days, which adds safety stock; there is no later data to score this forecast against, section 2 does that for an earlier cutoff.

## 5. Limits

- Only about 0.4% of demand is censored in this dataset, so differences between A and B are small overall and concentrated in the few series that ran out of stock.
- The hero stockout begins on the first day of the plain backtest, so there both A and B were trained before it. The stress test (cutoff on day 83) exists to cover that case; it only has 7 days of true demand left to score against.
- The scripted supply failures block deliveries for every policy, so no forecast can prevent those empty shelves.
- One simulated world and a few cutoffs: the intervals reflect resampling of series, not of worlds or of cutoffs.
- Daily demand per store and SKU is a small count with Poisson noise, so WAPE of 50-60% is normal at this level; the naive column is the yardstick.
- Open purchase orders are not tracked in the data, so `on_order` is 0 in the recommendations.
